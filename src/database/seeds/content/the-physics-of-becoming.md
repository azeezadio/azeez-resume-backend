Every morning, before the world gets a say in your day, you stand in front of your wardrobe and make a decision. It looks small. Blue shirt or black shirt. It is not small. I will come back to this, but first, let me tell you about a man and an apple.

The story goes that Isaac Newton was sitting under a tree when an apple fell, and instead of eating it like a normal person, he started asking why it fell down and not up, or sideways, or into another dimension. The answer he eventually wrote down changed everything. Every object with mass pulls every other object with mass. Everything. The apple pulls the earth, the earth pulls the apple. You, sitting there reading this, are pulling on the moon right now. Not much, but you are.

And the law says the strength of that pull depends on only two things. How heavy the things are, and how close they are to each other. Heavier things pull harder. Closer things pull harder. That is the whole law. A teenager can fit it on a sticky note.

Now here is my conjecture, and I am calling it a conjecture because I am an engineer, not a physicist, and I know at least one person will email me about units. I think this law is not just about planets. I think it is quietly running your life.

## Conviction has mass

I lead engineering teams for a living. I have interviewed hundreds of people, sat in more meetings than any human should, and watched careers rise and stall from close range. And the thing I keep noticing is this. Some people walk into a room and the room bends toward them. Not because they are loud. Not because they have the biggest title. It is because they believe something, deeply, and belief has weight.

A person with strong conviction is a heavy object. Ideas fall toward them. People fall toward them. Opportunities that were floating around aimlessly suddenly find an orbit. You have met this person. Maybe it is the colleague everyone somehow ends up asking for advice. Maybe it is your grandmother, who has never raised her voice in her life but whose one sentence can reorganize an entire family meeting.

Meanwhile, a person with no conviction is like dust. Dust has mass too, technically, but nothing orbits dust. Dust just goes wherever the wind is going. You have met this person too. Every opinion they hold was installed by the last person they spoke to. Ask them what they want and they check your face first, looking for a hint.

Here is the uncomfortable part. The universe does not reward you for being right. It rewards you for being heavy. Which brings me to the scariest paragraph in this article.

## Gravity does not check your heart

Newton's law has no morality clause. A heavy object pulls, full stop. It does not matter if the object is a hospital or a bomb.

And this is the thing about people who do terrible things. We like to imagine villains know they are villains, cackling in a dark room somewhere. But almost nobody wakes up and says, today I will be evil. The most dangerous people in history woke up feeling like heroes. Their conviction was enormous. Genuinely enormous. It was just pointed at the wrong star.

That is why they were dangerous, by the way. Not despite the conviction, because of it. A confused person with bad ideas is annoying. A deeply convinced person with bad ideas is a planet, and planets collect moons. People fell into their orbit not because those people were stupid, but because the pull was real. Gravity felt like truth. It always does from the inside.

So no, having strong conviction does not make you good. It just makes you heavy. The direction is a separate assignment, and it is graded separately. This is why the people I respect most do two things at once. They build their conviction like a muscle, and they check its direction like a paranoid pilot. Believing hard and auditing what you believe are not enemies. They are the two hands you need to carry anything worth carrying.

## Resonance, or why your friends are frequencies

There is a famous rule that soldiers must break step when crossing a bridge. Hundreds of soldiers marching in perfect rhythm can bring a bridge down, not because they are heavy, but because their rhythm matches the bridge's natural rhythm. Each small step lands at exactly the right moment to make the swing bigger. Push a child on a swing at the right moment every time and a small push becomes a big flight. Push at the wrong moments and you are just a confused adult slapping a swing.

That is resonance. Small forces, perfectly timed, become enormous.

And resonance does not only happen between people. It can happen inside one head. There is someone I learned a lot from over the years, Oj, who has this ability I have stopped trying to explain to outsiders. He can hold an enormous amount of information in his mind all at once. Systems, edge cases, the thing marketing said three weeks ago, the weird bug from last quarter, all of it live at the same time. In most heads that much information would be noise, a hundred instruments all playing their own song. In Oj's head it is a symphony. Everything is somehow in tune with everything else, and when he finally opens his mouth or ships the thing, the result is always beautiful. Not correct. Beautiful. There is a difference, and you know it when you see it. That is what happens when the parts of your knowledge resonate instead of just coexisting.

People resonate too. You know this feeling. You meet someone and within ten minutes you are finishing each other's sentences, and two hours disappear like NEPA took the light. That is not magic, that is frequency matching. And it works in both directions. The right person resonating with your ambition can swing you higher than you could ever push yourself. The wrong person resonating with your bitterness can also swing you, very efficiently, straight into a wall. Bitterness has a frequency. So does gossip. So does cynicism dressed up as realism. If a friendship only vibrates when you are complaining, you are not connecting, you are marching in step on a bridge.

This is why the room you spend your time in matters more than the seminar you attended once. Resonance needs repetition. One push does nothing. It is the small, matched pushes, over and over, that build the amplitude.

## Closer things pull harder

Back to Newton's sticky note. The pull weakens with distance, and it weakens fast. Double the distance and the pull does not drop by half, it drops to a quarter. Physics is dramatic like that.

Now apply that to your life and it explains something we all pretend not to know. Your dreams are far away. Your phone is right here. Your five year vision is far away. The colleague who complains at your desk every day is right here. And close things pull harder. Much harder.

This is why a person can sincerely want to become a writer and spend four hours a night watching other people live their lives on a screen. It is not weakness of character. It is physics. The distant thing, no matter how massive, is losing a gravity contest to the small thing sitting in your palm.

So the trick is not more motivation. Motivation is trying to out-shout gravity, and gravity does not have ears. The trick is moving things. Bring the thing you want to become closer, and push the thing that is eating you further away. The book on the pillow instead of the phone by the bed. The gym clothes laid out the night before. The one heavy, convicted friend you actually call every week instead of admire from afar. You are not fighting the law. You are using it. Engineers do not argue with gravity, we build bridges with it.

## The wardrobe, finally

Which brings me back to your wardrobe this morning.

Choosing what to wear feels like the least cosmic decision available to a human being. But watch what it actually is. It is you, before anyone else has voted, deciding who is showing up today. Some days you dress like someone going to win. Some days you dress like someone going to hide. Your body notices. Other people's gravity fields notice too.

And showing up itself, the plain, boring act of appearing, is the same move at a larger size. Every time you show up, to the work, to the hard conversation, to the gym, to the friendship, you add a little mass to the version of you that shows up. Every time you don't, you add mass to the version that doesn't. There is no neutral day. You are always feeding one of them, and whichever one gets heavier starts pulling the future toward itself.

I have watched this on engineering teams for years. The engineer who becomes indispensable is almost never the genius. It is the one who kept appearing. Kept writing things down, kept asking, kept shipping small things while the geniuses were waiting for a big enough problem. Ten years later everyone calls it talent. It was accumulation. Mass, added daily, in the same direction.

## So, the conjecture

I should confess where this article really started. There is someone I have worked with for years, Ik, and Ik loves Isaac Newton the way other people love football clubs. Bring up Newton around him and you have surrendered your next thirty minutes. Now, none of this was new to me. I sat through the same physics classes everyone did, and the law was just another thing you memorized to pass an exam and then returned to the teacher on your way out. But hearing a grown man speak about a three hundred year old scientist with that much love made me pick the law up again, turn it over, and actually look at it. And this time I saw a mirror instead of a formula. This article is basically me catching what Ik has.

So here it is in one place. You are always being pulled. By people, by habits, by beliefs, by whatever is heaviest and closest to you right now. There is no version of life where you float free, so the only real question is the one nobody asks out loud. What am I currently falling toward?

You cannot switch gravity off. But you can do four things. You can get heavier, by building conviction on purpose instead of inheriting it by accident. You can check your direction, ruthlessly, because heavy and wrong is the most dangerous combination on earth. You can choose your resonance, by picking the frequencies you march in step with. And you can move things, bringing what you want to become close enough to win the daily pull.

Becoming is not a lightning bolt. Nobody transforms on a Tuesday afternoon. Becoming is orbital. It is thousands of small pulls, most of them invisible, slowly bending your path until one day you look up and you are somewhere, and you are someone, and it feels sudden but it wasn't.

The apple was always going to fall. The only question was which way was down.

Choose your down carefully. Then get dressed like you mean it.

And Ik, you were right about Newton. Please do not let this get to your head.
